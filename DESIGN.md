# Primordia: design notes

Design decisions, deviations from `primorida_spec.md`, the rules added beyond it, and how the GPU
pipeline and telemetry work. The [README](README.md) covers usage.

## GPU-resident, pipelined execution (default)

The whole state stays on the GPU: `phys.wgsl` ports every phase except interventions and vent bookkeeping,
and `exec.wgsl` ports the interpreter. Each epoch is one command buffer, and up to 6 epochs are in flight.
Tallies and births come back through a ring of staging buffers mapped asynchronously. Whether the bodies
phase runs is decided on the GPU (indirect dispatch gated by the vote count; union-find labelling rounds
gated by a "changed" flag), so no epoch waits on a read-back. Telemetry, run-directory hashes and snapshots,
the viewers and the web feed run on an observer thread from asynchronous state snapshots.

`--exec-only` is the original split: the interpreter on the GPU, every other phase on the CPU (rayon,
deterministic), with the state uploaded and downloaded every epoch. `--cpu` runs the CPU golden model,
which implements every phase. `gpu/tests/parity.rs` checks both GPU modes bit for bit against it,
including the pipelined/asynchronous path. `PRIMORDIA_PROFILE=1 primordia bench` breaks GPU time down
by phase.

| World (RTX 5070) | interpreter on GPU only | resident | resident + pipelined + kernel work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2D 1023² full physics | 1.8 ep/s | 18 | **48** |
| 2D 501² full physics | 8.7 | 41 | **127** |
| 3D 99³ full physics | 1.8 | 9.8 | **34** |
| paper-0d (8192 steps) | 9.7 | 10.6 | **32** |

GPU utilisation during a full run with viewer, telemetry and web feed: 95–98%.

## Design decisions and deviations from the spec

* **Single source of truth.** Layout constants, parameter indices and rotation tables are generated by
  `primordia_core::wgsl::constants()` and prepended to the shaders when the pipelines are created, rather
  than by a `build.rs`.
* **Mirror symmetry of numeric code.** `RD`/`WR` move byte *values* through the stack, and integer
  arithmetic does not commute with `b ^ 1`, so a byte-level physics cannot be exactly mirror-symmetric.
  As the spec does for RNG draws, the twin world reads byte values in the reflected frame:
  `RD` pushes `mirror(b)`, `WR` writes `mirror(v)`, and bit-flip mutations apply `M∘flip∘M`. The twin test
  therefore proves that the *implementation* (geometry, orientations, scheduler, RNG keys, transport
  dithers, chemistry hands, bodies) has no hidden asymmetry. The encoding asymmetry of numeric
  code is a property of PISA-1 itself: L-vs-D results have to be judged statistically across seeds
  (`batch` reports the binomial test), and C0 (`stereo = off`) is the baseline.
* **PITCH_L / PITCH_D** are the same rotation. A rotation about the lateral axis involves only facing and
  up, so it is achiral; making the two different would break the mirror requirement (T2).
* **Conflicts.** Anything that reads beyond the executor's Chebyshev-1 neighbourhood (shielding counts
  the target's other neighbours) reads a flag snapshot taken at the start of the sub-step, so parallel
  executors never race. An individual that moves ends its slice, and each individual executes at most
  once per epoch (`FL_EXEC`).
* **Transport.** The flux is computed once per face from both endpoints with a 16-bit Philox dither; the
  mirror twin complements the dither on x faces, which makes the flux exactly antisymmetric under
  reflection. Outflows are limited proportionally so that no cell goes negative (still exactly
  conservative). Up to 8 fields share one Philox call per face; L/D pairs are never split across batches,
  so hand-swapped dither slots line up in the twin.
* **Mutation sampling** uses a Poisson inverse CDF (series for small λ, a 64k-entry `exp(-λ)` table
  otherwise) instead of a binomial table.
* **Halting.** A halted cell wakes once its energy reaches `energy.wake_energy` (default 256); waking at
  `E > 0` would make a cell restart its slice from IP 0 forever with too little energy to finish anything.
* **paper-2d** uses the checkerboard scheduler with face neighbours, not the paper's radius-2 random-claim
  scheduler. paper-0d/2d use `step_budget = 8192`, as cubff does.
* **Defaults that the spec leaves open:** jumps, `PUSH`, fillers and `JREL` cost 1; `DIGEST` costs 2 per
  byte; `ENRG`/`FREE` scale by 16; `TEMP` = clamp(K − 200, 0, 255); sunlight heats by 0.02 (0.01 K units) per
  unit of light; κ defaults are 0.08 so that κ × faces ≤ ½ also holds in 3D.
* **Organism candidates** are approximated as bonded bodies of size ≥ 2 whose members descend from one
  parent lineage. The census clusters at most 20,000 sampled tapes.
* **Viewer.** The native viewer renders on the CPU into textures (not straight from GPU buffers). "Step
  sub-step" runs one epoch on the CPU golden model and lets you scrub through the 3^D sub-step frames.
  Branching from the viewer writes a snapshot plus a diff template for `primordia branch`.

## Rules added beyond the spec

All default to the spec's behaviour, run identically on the CPU and the GPU, and are covered
by golden and parity tests. They came out of the long live runs, in this order:

| Setting | Rule | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `individual.starve_after` | a cell dormant (zero energy) for this many epochs without a break dies | dormant cells never died, so the world filled up and turnover stopped |
| `energy.sun.moon` | night-time light never falls below this fraction of full sun | with dark nights, sleeping only slowed a cell's decline; weak moonlight (0.02) lets a sleeping cell break even while awake cells still starve |
| `individual.max_age` | a cell dies once it is this many epochs old | keeps turnover going if cells learn to survive the night |
| `individual.sleep_scale` | `SLEEP n` lasts min(n, 255) × scale epochs | at most 31 epochs per call, so covering a 1,000-epoch night took dozens of calls |
| `individual.sleep_shield` | neighbours cannot write to or digest a sleeping cell's tape (reads are allowed) | sleepers survived the night but waking neighbours copied over their programs, so sleeping could not be inherited |
| `individual.sleep_absorb` | a sleeping cell keeps this fraction of its light absorption | with protection alone, constant short daytime naps took over (97% of cells asleep) and replaced bonded bodies; a sleeper with no income pays for sleeping by day but hardly at night |

`runs/live-m4.toml` uses all six (starve 800, moon 0.02, lifespan 6,000, sleep ×16, shield on, sleepers
absorb nothing). `runs/live-m1.toml` to `live-m3.toml` are the earlier steps.

## Telemetry

* **Plot history.** Every live metric is kept as up to 20,000 buckets of mean / min / max over the whole
  run, merged pairwise when full. Plots never drop samples, so the day/night swing cannot alias into false
  trends. The history is saved as `history.json` beside the snapshots and carried over by `--resume`.
* **Day/night bookkeeping.** Sunset is at half the day period and sunrise at 0. At each sunrise the
  recorder logs night survival (sunrise ÷ sunset population), births during the day (exact, from the
  birth stream), deaths (births minus population change), and the asleep / dormant shares and largest
  body at dawn (`days.parquet`).
* **Cluster persistence** (`telemetry.cluster_every`, default 250 epochs; `clusters.parquet`). Two kinds
  of cluster with at least 50 cells are tracked: bonded bodies, and kin clusters (connected patches of
  neighbours whose programs share at least 12 of their first 16 bytes). A cluster continues one from the
  previous sample when at least 25% of the earlier cluster's cells are still alive inside it (same site,
  birth epoch and lineage). A cluster that continues across a sunrise adds a night. Tracking restarts on
  `--resume`.
* **Cell states** (`life.parquet`, every 10 epochs): sunlight, asleep, dormant, SLEEP carriers, bonded,
  mean age, and shares older than one and two days.
* **Behaviour.** The frequency of 23 behaviour instructions in living programs, relative to a random
  byte (1×), is a live series; the full 256-opcode histogram goes to `opcodes.parquet`.
