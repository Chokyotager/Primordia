# Primordia: design notes

Design decisions, deviations from `primorida_spec.md`, the rules added beyond it, and how the GPU
pipeline and telemetry work. The [README](README.md) covers usage.

## GPU-resident, pipelined execution (default)

The whole state stays on the GPU: `phys.wgsl` ports every phase except interventions and vent bookkeeping,
and `exec.wgsl` ports the interpreter. Each epoch is one command buffer, and up to 6 epochs are in flight.
Tallies and births come back through a ring of staging buffers mapped asynchronously. Whether the bodies
phase runs is decided on the GPU (indirect dispatch gated by the vote count; union-find labelling rounds
gated by a "changed" flag), so no epoch waits on a read-back. Telemetry, run-directory hashes and snapshots,
the viewers and the web feed run on an observer thread from asynchronous state snapshots.

`--exec-only` is the original split: the interpreter on the GPU, every other phase on the CPU (rayon,
deterministic), with the state uploaded and downloaded every epoch. `--cpu` runs the CPU golden model,
which implements every phase. `gpu/tests/parity.rs` checks both GPU modes bit for bit against it,
including the pipelined/asynchronous path. `PRIMORDIA_PROFILE=1 primordia bench` breaks GPU time down
per kernel with GPU timestamps (where the adapter supports them; otherwise by synchronised phase), and
reports the GPU span of an epoch. `bench` counts epochs still in flight, so compare runs over the same
epoch window, and preferably with `run` over a fixed resumed window.

Throughput notes (M17, 501², RTX 3050, a day-3 morning window: 20.7 epochs/s at step budget 256). The
interpreter is ~75% of GPU time and is compute-bound by divergence: at midday 92% of cells run a slice
averaging ~120 steps (~25 M steps per epoch), so warps execute many different instruction paths. The flux
scratch arrays (`jf`, `bo`, `outs`) are field-major; cell-major they made transport 3.3× slower (22 →
6.7 ms per epoch). Tried without gain and removed: compacting each colour's live cells before dispatch
(and running 1–8 per warp), range-first decoding, a one-word tape cache, costs in workgroup memory, and
workgroup sizes 32/128. A parallel CPU interpreter would manage ~36 M steps/s per core (the GPU does ~730 M).

| World (RTX 5070) | interpreter on GPU only | resident | resident + pipelined + kernel work |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2D 1023² full physics | 1.8 ep/s | 18 | **48** |
| 2D 501² full physics | 8.7 | 41 | **127** |
| 3D 99³ full physics | 1.8 | 9.8 | **34** |
| paper-0d (8192 steps) | 9.7 | 10.6 | **32** |

GPU utilisation during a full run with viewer, telemetry and web feed: 95–98%.

## Design decisions and deviations from the spec

* **Single source of truth.** Layout constants, parameter indices and rotation tables are generated by
  `primordia_core::wgsl::constants()` and prepended to the shaders when the pipelines are created, rather
  than by a `build.rs`.
* **Mirror symmetry and mutation.** Numeric `RD`/`WR` still use the reflected frame in twin
  runs because integer arithmetic on opcode values does not commute with the mirror map.
  The twin test checks that transformed implementation; it is not a statistical neutrality test
  of arbitrary ordinary programs. Bit-flip errors now choose equally between the raw bit-flip
  kernel and its mirror conjugate, in ordinary runs as well as twins. The random frame is XORed
  with the twin flag to preserve exact paired trajectories. Exhaustive transition-count tests
  verify `K(Ma, Mb) = K(a, b)`, including empty-byte transitions, and mutation-only copier
  populations check for the former D bias. This changes the mutation law deliberately; it does
  not establish that every evolved numeric program or every run is unbiased.
* **PITCH_L / PITCH_D** are the same rotation. A rotation about the lateral axis involves only facing and
  up, so it is achiral; making the two different would break the mirror requirement (T2).
* **Conflicts.** Anything that reads beyond the executor's Chebyshev-1 neighbourhood (shielding counts
  the target's other neighbours) reads a flag snapshot taken at the start of the sub-step, so parallel
  executors never race. An individual that moves ends its slice, and each individual executes at most
  once per epoch (`FL_EXEC`).
* **Transport.** The flux is computed once per face from both endpoints with a 16-bit Philox dither; the
  mirror twin complements the dither on x faces, which makes the flux exactly antisymmetric under
  reflection. Outflows are limited proportionally so that no cell goes negative (still exactly
  conservative); the limiting ratio rounds down, so the limited flows never overdraw. Bonded energy
  equalisation is the same explicit step, so validation requires `share_beta` × faces ≤ 1 (0.25 in 2D). Up to 8 fields share one Philox call per face; L/D pairs are never split across batches,
  so hand-swapped dither slots line up in the twin.
* **Mutation sampling** caps each channel at 64 events per cell and epoch. Means up to 4 use
  the small-mean inverse-CDF recurrence; larger means use precomputed Q32 CDFs on a 1/64 grid
  with integer interpolation, shared exactly by CPU and GPU. Interpolation mixes adjacent
  Poissons and adds at most 1/16384 to the uncapped variance. At means of 128 or more, the
  probability of fewer than 64 events is below Q32 resolution, so the result is 64. The shared
  tables are cached. This avoids the old intermediate overflow and exponential underflow.
* **Halting.** A halted cell wakes once its energy reaches `energy.wake_energy` (default 256); waking at
  `E > 0` would make a cell restart its slice from IP 0 forever with too little energy to finish anything.
* **paper-2d** uses the checkerboard scheduler with face neighbours, not the paper's radius-2 random-claim
  scheduler. paper-0d/2d use `step_budget = 8192`, as cubff does.
* **Defaults that the spec leaves open:** jumps, `PUSH`, fillers and `JREL` cost 1; `DIGEST` costs 2 per
  byte; `ENRG`/`FREE` scale by 16; `TEMP` = clamp(K − 200, 0, 255); sunlight heats by 0.02 (0.01 K units) per
  unit of light; κ defaults are 0.08 so that κ × faces ≤ ½ also holds in 3D.
* **Organism candidates** are approximated as bonded bodies of size ≥ 2 whose members descend from one
  parent lineage. The census clusters at most 20,000 sampled tapes.
* **Viewer.** The native viewer renders on the CPU into textures (not straight from GPU buffers). "Step
  sub-step" runs one epoch on the CPU golden model and lets you scrub through the 3^D sub-step frames.
  Branching from the viewer writes a snapshot plus a diff template for `primordia branch`.

## Correctness and checkpoint semantics

* `DIVIDE` accepts a one-past-end H1 for a suffix or whole-tape transfer. STEP costs use their own
  opcode overrides. `SLEEP n` skips exactly `min(n,255) * sleep_scale` future execution rounds;
  its final sleeping round retains the sleep shield even after the counter reaches zero.
* Live edits refresh GPU neighbors, transport plans, reaction tables, and image mutation maps.
  Changes to dimensions, axis lengths, tape capacity, or chemical/signal layout are rejected.
  The shared model supports at most 16 reactions; unsupported counts fail validation.
* Transport plans size to the configured fields. Vent uploads aggregate positions without losing
  duplicate sources. Birth buffers hold one record per site, the maximum number of newborns
  that can be present at the epoch's birth-recording phase.
* Cell headers are 21 words: the original 9, the resume context (`H_CTX`, `H_H0`, `H_H1`, 8 stack words)
  and the reproduction rest counter (`H_REST`). Version 3 `.psnap` files store all 21; versions 1 and 2
  load with the extension empty.
* Snapshots carry their own effective configuration and accounting to the observer. Version 2
  `.psnap` files preserve pending tallies, residuals, violation history, and light, and a sibling
  `.toml` saves the effective configuration. Version 1 remains readable; its missing accounting
  requires rebasing on the restored arrays.
* Resume and branch save a local origin snapshot plus replay metadata in a fresh run directory.
  Older checkpoints without configuration sidecars recover prior valid `set` events from their
  run log. Existing nonempty output directories and missing explicit event files are errors.
* Birth events are bucketed by epoch and delivered before their completed snapshots. Day boundaries
  are sampled even between ordinary metrics samples. Changing the day period starts a new partial
  accounting interval; resumed runs likewise begin with a partial day if resumed between sunrises.
* Region reflection moves walls, environmental fields, light, vents, orientation, bonds, votes,
  and pending adhesion requests together. It cuts existing bonds crossing the region boundary.
* Minimized assay results are retested after trimming; NOP-prefixed tapes remain eligible for kin
  clusters, and a cluster split gives the old identity to only one descendant. The optional GPU
  takeover experiment synchronizes the state before measuring the final tape population.

**Fixes from the November 2026 code scan** (regression tests in `tests/lifecycle.rs`, telemetry
`tests/regressions.rs` and GPU parity):

* Resumed execution: an op that pops its operand before paying (`TAKE`, `ALLOC`, `DIGEST`) puts it back
  when the payment fails, so the retry sees the same stack; the saved IP has 24 signed bits (a long
  `JREL` could overflow the old 16); snapshot version 4 converts version 3 contexts on load. A region
  mirror, or switching `individual.resume` off in a live edit, discards saved contexts.
* `energy.copy_rest` starts only when a byte is actually written into another cell (not on a write that
  fails for want of monomers).
* Transport: bonded faces conduct `bonds.gamma` times faster but are capped at the stability bound
  (κ·faces ≤ ½); with the default γ = 4 and κ_c = 0.05, chemistry and monomers inside bonded bodies used
  to oscillate between 0 and twice the mean (M17 included). The GPU read γ = 0 as "no γ"; γ is now
  validated to 0..=1000.
* Live `set` edits close the ledger interval under the old rules and rebase it on the new ones.
* `--set` and batch overrides merge table values into their section and are validated once, after all
  of them. `batch` refuses an output directory that already holds results; the final epoch always
  reaches telemetry (stale summaries with `snapshot_every = 0`); `branch` keeps the parent's later
  interventions (not its later `set` events); `bench --snapshot` uses the snapshot's effective config.
* Telemetry: the replicator assay and executed repertoire run with a budget of 16 steps per tape byte
  (one slice at a resume world's small budget missed long replicators, M17's ancestor included);
  sampled measures spread their samples over the whole population; empty newborns are not a role.

The fixes change deterministic trajectories. Continuing a legacy checkpoint uses the corrected rules;
reproducing a historical run's old hashes requires the source revision in its manifest. The corrected
seed templates allocate 15 bytes for their 15-byte program. A separate controlled regression proves
parent → child → grandchild reproduction after offspring isolation; it does not assert spontaneous
multicellular differentiation under the live preset.

## Rules added beyond the spec

All default to the spec's behaviour, run identically on the CPU and the GPU, and are covered
by golden and parity tests. They came out of the long live runs, in this order:

| Setting | Rule | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `individual.starve_after` | a cell dormant (zero energy) for this many epochs without a break dies | dormant cells never died, so the world filled up and turnover stopped |
| `energy.sun.moon` | night-time light never falls below this fraction of full sun | with dark nights, sleeping only slowed a cell's decline; weak moonlight (0.02) lets a sleeping cell break even while awake cells still starve |
| `individual.max_age` | a cell dies once it is this many epochs old | keeps turnover going if cells learn to survive the night |
| `individual.max_age_spread` | each individual's lifespan is max_age × (1 ± spread), uniform, fixed at birth from its lineage ID (no extra state) | M18 filled its world within 400 epochs, and that cohort died together every 20,000 epochs: a 30% population crash followed by recolonisation by a few lineages, a sawtooth in high-order entropy |
| `individual.sleep_scale` | `SLEEP n` lasts min(n, 255) × scale epochs | at most 31 epochs per call, so covering a 1,000-epoch night took dozens of calls |
| `individual.sleep_shield` | neighbours cannot write to or digest a sleeping cell's tape (reads are allowed) | sleepers survived the night but waking neighbours copied over their programs, so sleeping could not be inherited |
| `individual.sleep_absorb` | a sleeping cell keeps this fraction of its light absorption | with protection alone, constant short daytime naps took over (97% of cells asleep) and replaced bonded bodies; a sleeper with no income pays for sleeping by day but hardly at night |
| `individual.sleep_upkeep` | a sleeping cell pays this fraction of its upkeep (default 1/8, the spec's value) | in the M5 live run a copier that sleeps after every copied byte (`CPY SLEEP` in its loop) took over on day 14: 85% of cells asleep, bodies broken up |
| `energy.vents.stagger` | the founding vents start at random ages below their lifetime | in M11 all six were created at epoch 0 with a 100-day lifetime and switched off together on day 100, killing every long-lived body |
| `bonds.mutual` | `BOND`/`BONDS_L`/`BONDS_D` leave a request; the bond forms only when the neighbour bonds back before the requester's next slice (a request lapses after an epoch without a slice) | in M12 bonded neighbours were no more related than unbonded ones, so bodies were aggregates of strangers; a bond also lets the partner write past shielding |
| `bonds.kin_only` | `BOND` joins two cells only if their programs are kin (75% of the filled bytes among the first 16 slots match, at least 4); birth bonds are exempt | in M15 mutual adhesion still let any two cells carrying `BOND` stick, and one chimeric mat bonded ~80% of the world |
| `bonds.birth_bond` | a child made by `ALLOC` or `DIVIDE` stays bonded to its parent with this probability | lets bodies grow clonally, the usual route to multicellularity |
| `individual.dormant_idle` | the dormancy counter behind `starve_after` counts only epochs in which a halted cell did not execute | a copier that runs until it is out of energy ends every slice halted, so busy, well-fed cells counted as starving |
| `individual.dormant_grace`, `dormant_absorb`, `dormant_rot` | once dormant for the grace period, a cell keeps only this fraction of its light absorption and its tape decays this many times faster | a dormant cell paid nothing, kept absorbing and woke every ~85 epochs even by moonlight, so dormancy was a free way to sit out the night; now only a bonded neighbour's shared energy can rescue it |
| `shielding.light_cover` | how much face cover (bonds, occupied neighbours) reduces light absorption; radiation, decay and write shielding keep the full cover | in 2D the sun is overhead, yet every bond cost a cell a quarter of its light, which taxed bodies |
| `individual.resume` | a slice continues where the previous one stopped: the IP, both heads and the 16-entry stack are kept in the cell's header (unreduced, so addresses follow the current partner). `HALT`, a stack fault or an empty address space start the next slice afresh; running out of energy retries the instruction, `SLEEP` and a move continue after it. Spatial worlds only | every slice restarted at IP 0, so a copy that needed more than one slice's energy recopied the same prefix forever and genomes could not outgrow one slice (the audit's 64-byte probe stalled at 51 bytes; M16 under the corrected code collapsed to ~16-byte copiers by day 12) |
| `bonds.protect`, `bonds.construct_window` | a bonded neighbour may write into or digest a cell only during the cell's first `construct_window` epochs (default 256); after that bonds still share energy and shield the cell from outsiders | a bond let any bonded copier overwrite its partner without shielding, so bodies protected copiers better than the specialists inside them |
| `energy.copy_rest`, `energy.copy_absorb` | for `copy_rest` epochs after a reproductive act (a permitted write into another cell, `ALLOC` or `DIVIDE`) a cell keeps only `copy_absorb` of its automatic absorption | every cell absorbed light and vent energy whatever it did, so a lone copier could do everything a specialist could. This germ-soma trade-off lets bonded absorbers that do not copy feed a copier through energy sharing |
| `shielding.private` | another cell may write into (or digest) a tape only during its first `bonds.construct_window` epochs, while its parent builds it; after that only the cell writes its own tape | M17 (995k epochs) had no heredity of whole genomes: 191k of 193k cells had unique tapes, and a neighbour-spraying loop without `ALLOC` filled 82% of cells; overwriting plus damage erased genomes faster than selection acted |
| `shielding.predation` | with `private`: `DIGEST` may eat an established cell, but only whole (n at least its tape length): the tape is erased, the eater gains its monomers and the cell dies at the end of the epoch; writes stay blocked and ordinary shielding and `bonds.protect` still apply | M18's full world of protected cells could only turn over through old age; killing (unlike overwriting) keeps genomes inherited or destroyed whole and lets a cell make room for its offspring |
| `shielding.parent_only` | with `private`: a cell under construction is open only to its parent (the lineage it records as parent); others can at most eat it whole | under plain privacy every copier sprayed its genome, misaligned, into all young neighbours (M19: 99% unique tapes) |
| `shielding.gestation` | a cell with a parent does not execute until its construction window is over (it still absorbs and pays upkeep) | a half-built child ran its partial program, `ALLOC` included, and multiplied incomplete genomes |
| `[tasks]` `enabled`, `reward` | `IN` (0x7D) pushes an 8-bit input (keyed by cell, epoch and count); `OUT` (0x7E) pops a value, and a correct logic function of the last two inputs (NOT; AND, ADD, SUB; OR, NAND; XOR, NOR; echoing an input earns nothing) earns reward × 2^(level−1) new energy, booked like catalysis through the site's reaction counter so the ledger balances, each task once per slice; `tasks.steps` can also extend the slice. Fillers when off | Avida-style computation rewards, the best-documented route to open-ended growth in digital evolution |
| `shielding.armor` | `HARDEN` (0x7F, cost via `[costs]`) arms the executing cell; an attack that would eat it whole only strips the armour. A filler when off | an arms race: M21 evolved a whole-cell predator, but prey had no defence |
| `bonds.colony_generations` | a birth bond joins a child to its parent only within this many generations of the colony's founder (colony generation in flag bits 30–31); the next child is born free and founds a colony | a colony life cycle: colonies grow, then release propagules, so they can compete as units |
| `energy.sun.season_band` | a band of extra light, 1 + band·cos(2π(y/ny − t/season_period)), crosses the world once per season | a changing environment that rewards sensing and moving |

The last three are new in M17 and default off (`copy_rest` 0). Options added after configuration hashes
were recorded are left out of the serialized configuration at their defaults, so older configurations and
the hashes in their snapshots are unchanged. `individual.pair_order` and `individual.random_initial_facing`
are the spec's own settings; M17 is the first live world to use `self_first` and fixed facing.

`runs/live-m4.toml` uses the first six (starve 800, moon 0.02, lifespan 6,000, sleep ×16, shield on, sleepers
absorb nothing). `runs/live-m5.toml` is M4 with `shielding.s_bond` 0.4 and `energy.share_beta` 0.125; `runs/live-m6.toml` adds
`individual.sleep_upkeep` 0.5 (the running M6 world was resumed from M5's day-11 snapshot). The sleeping
copier still took over M6, between days 18 and 56, and held about 90% of cells asleep for the next 360
days: a cell asleep most of the time was nearly immune to being overwritten, so it could afford to copy
slowly. `runs/live-m7.toml` turns `sleep_shield` off again, so a sleeper is protected only by ordinary
shielding from its neighbours, which bonding strengthens. In M7 sleeping vanished and bonding was strongly
selected (BOND 10–17× a random byte, 60–70% of cells bonded), but each day the world filled with one
giant mat (~150k cells) and about 75% of cells died each night, since moonlight (~3 per epoch) could not
cover upkeep (~7–11). `runs/live-m8.toml` raises the moonlight to 0.06: in its first 8 days about 75% of
cells survived each night (M7: ~25%) and bodies of 30k–70k cells lived to dawn (M7: 2k–8k).
`runs/live-m9.toml` sets the moonlight to 0.04, just below an idle cell's upkeep, and makes vents release
20× more free energy (8,000 per epoch, heat unchanged). Vents were about 0.02% of the world's energy and
no tracked body sat near one; now each can carry about 900 cells through the night. In its first 18 days
about half the cells survived each night and a few small bodies (58–700 cells) sat within 11 cells of a
vent, but none lasted more than one night. `runs/live-m10.toml` makes vents release 200,000 per epoch
(500× the default, heat unchanged; enough for about 33,000 cells at night, with ~8 vents about 14% of the
solar energy) and last 100 days, spawning 4× less often so that about 8 exist at a time.

M10 settled by day 20. Each vent became an oasis about 25 cells across (at dawn 77% of its sites occupied
against 47% elsewhere, programs of 36–79 bytes against ~20, ~2 million stored energy per cell near the vent,
only +45 K at the vent itself), but no body lasted more than 3–4 nights and differentiation stayed flat (~19%
of bodies with 2+ cell types, ~21-byte programs). The initial vents also last 100 days, so the count rose to
14 before settling. `runs/live-m11.toml` lengthens the lifespan from 3 to 10 days (`max_age` 20,000).

Vents and stored energy: a cell sitting on a vent stores at most about lifespan × absorption × the free energy
at the vent (~900,000 at 200,000 per epoch); with a 10-day lifespan that is ~9 × 10⁸, about 5× below the u32
limit. Saturating adds past it would lose energy and the ledger check would report it.

In M11 the bodies at vents became long-lived: several a day survived 3+ nights and the record reached 32
nights (M10: 3–4), while bonding elsewhere was selected against (BOND 0.1–0.2× a random byte, a third of
cells bonded). All six founding vents expired together on day 100 and the long-lived bodies died with them;
from day 93 a strongly bonding lineage spread (BOND 5–14×, giant mats again) with more differentiation
(23–25% of bodies with 2+ cell types against 12–16%, 30–39-byte programs). `runs/live-m12.toml` staggers
the founding vents (`energy.vents.stagger`).

M12 ran 533 days. Its long-lived vent bodies peaked at 27 nights around day 85; from day 77 the same
bonding lineage took over (it did again from M12's day-71.5 snapshot with a new seed, and 10× thermal bond
breaking only delayed it by 15–20 days), and the world then stayed flat for 430 days. Snapshot analysis
showed why "bodies" did not become organisms: bonded neighbours shared 8.3–8.4 of their first 16 program
bytes, unbonded neighbours 8.5 (random pairs 2–5), so bonding was indiscriminate. Programs sat near 20
bytes, about where ~6 × 10⁻⁴ damage per byte and epoch (decay 3 × 10⁻⁴, background mutation 2.4 × 10⁻⁴,
radiation) gives one hit per genome per generation; near vents, with constant copying, they reached 94–112
bytes against the 128-byte cap. `runs/live-m13.toml` adds mutual adhesion and birth bonds, cuts decay to
5 × 10⁻⁵ and background mutation to 10⁻⁴, and allows 256-byte genomes (step budget 1024, per-byte upkeep 0.06).

In M13 the sleeping lineage took over again from day 12, now without the sleep shield: with decay cut
sixfold a sleeper's program survived the night intact (80–90% of cells asleep, BOND gone from programs,
kin bodies down from ~200 to ~20, no body past 5 nights). The best program family did hold 60–120 conserved
bytes, against M12's best of ~49. `runs/live-m14.toml` shortens sleeps (`sleep_scale` 16 → 4).

M14 removed the sleeping strategy (about 0% of cells asleep for 70 days, night survival a steady 71–74%) but
settled into a world of 17-byte programs led by one copier family (`CPY ENRG ALLOC H0+ H1+ JMP -5`), small
short-lived bodies, and bonded neighbours no more related than unbonded ones (27% each with the
padding-free kin rule). Its sites held about 2,300 energy of unused X1+X2: CAT was 0.006% of program bytes,
since an unfolded CAT nets only ~3 energy and light recharged the store by ~0.6 per site and epoch.
`runs/live-m15.toml` raises `chemistry.n_cat` to 16 and the photolysis rate to 2e-2, so the chemical store
becomes a daylight reserve that only programs running CAT can spend, worth ~10× more to a folded catalyst.

M15 followed M14 for 150 days (17-byte programs, CAT selected out to ~0.01× a random byte) and then the
bonding lineage took over: from day 150 about 80% of cells were bonded into one 175k–194k-cell mat whose
bonded neighbours were less related than unbonded ones (21% vs 24% kin). Mutual adhesion only kept out cells
that do not run `BOND`. The chemical store stayed unused; its ceiling (~1,000 energy per site per day) was
too small against copying speed. `runs/live-m16.toml` adds like-with-like adhesion (`bonds.kin_only`) and a
10× larger store (`chemistry.init` 400/400/200, ~24,000 energy per site).

After the post-audit correctness fixes, M16 was rerun from the same seed (`runs/live-m16-s2-fix`, 53 days).
Handedness stayed within ±0.02 (the old run drifted to +0.05 in 6 days), but programs shrank from ~55 bytes
on day 6 to ~16 bytes by day 12 and stayed there: a daily cycle between ~168k cells at dawn and a full world
by noon (night survival ~67%), bodies of a few hundred cells, and falling program diversity. The audit's
lifecycle findings (`feedback.md`, sections 1–5) predicted this. `runs/live-m17.toml` adds all four
changes: own program first with fixed facing, resumed execution, protected bonds and the reproduction rest
period (`copy_rest` 100, `copy_absorb` 0.2). The old seed's copy loop never ends, which under resumed
execution means one child per cell, so M17 is seeded with a new 20-byte ancestor (`runs/seed-m17.jsonl`):
it resets its heads, turns at random, allocates a child of its own length, copies itself with a counter
on the stack and starts over. `tests/lifecycle.rs` checks it for three isolated generations. M17 also lowers the
step budget from 1,024 to 128: with resumed execution the budget limits a cell's work per epoch, not the
length of a genome it can copy, and the interpreter dominates GPU time (on a day-3 window: 9 epochs/s at
1,024, 15 at 256 before the transport layout fix, 21 at 256 after it).

M17 ran 995k epochs without whole-genome heredity (see the `shielding.private` row above).
`runs/live-m18.toml` adds genome privacy, cuts ambient damage tenfold (background mutation and radiation
10⁻⁵, hand flips 10⁻⁶, decay 5 × 10⁻⁶; copy errors unchanged) and starts from an empty world seeded with
M17's `ALLOC` ancestor (a random soup could not be overwritten and held its sites for its 10-day
lifespan). In a 3-day trial the ancestor filled the world within 400 epochs and 76–78% of cells survived
each night. It runs at ~45 epochs/s on the RTX 3050 in its first days. Its first 54,000 epochs (kept as
`runs/live-m18-s2-fixedage`) showed the fixed lifespan's synchronised die-offs, so M18 restarted with
lifespans spread ±25% (`individual.max_age_spread`). After 166k epochs (83 days) M18 held ~242k cells at dawn, tape length had
levelled off near 74 bytes and the largest bodies had 13–42 cells. `runs/live-m19.toml` is M18 with
predation (`shielding.predation`). Its first run (79k epochs, kept as `runs/live-m19-s2-cw256`) showed that both M18 and
M19 had mostly half-built genomes: parents copy slowly (the reproduction rest period starves them), and
children were ~37% filled when the 256-epoch construction window closed and privacy locked them. Two
thirds of cells were under half full, 231k of 245k tapes were unique, and the only bonds were birth
bonds between parent and child (bodies of at most 12). M19 restarted with a 2,000-epoch window.

**Heredity sweeps (November 2026).** 201² worlds from the `ALLOC` ancestor (`runs/seed-m17-201.jsonl`),
20 days, two seeds per variant, measured with `primordia analyze`. M19's rules (any neighbour may build a
newborn) collapsed to ~400 cells with two of three seeds and kept 95% of tapes unique. Parent-only
construction kept 33k–40k cells; adding gestation (256-epoch window) gave the best high-order entropy
(1.0 / 1.4). Neither the reproduction rest period nor copy errors (÷10) changed much. Ambient damage
did: at M19's rate a 35-byte genome took ~17 hits per lifetime. Dividing it by 10 raised HOE to ~2.5 at
day 20, with ~50% unique tapes and ~40% related neighbours (copy errors unchanged; ÷10 copy errors as
well gave 2.7 / 3.0). Over 60 days HOE then drifts down (3.4 → 1.5) as lineages diversify, while programs
lengthen (20 → 28 bytes) and self-copiers triple. `runs/live-m20.toml` is M19 with parent-only
construction, gestation, a 256-epoch window and ambient damage ÷10. Follow-up sweeps (M20 rules): bonding every child to its parent
(`birth_bond` 1.0) gave dozens of 10+-cell colonies (M20 rules: 1–2) and slightly higher HOE. Over 40 days
at M20's damage HOE eroded 2.7 → 2.3 while programs grew 22 → 26 bytes (the live M20 fell to 1.4 by day
75); with ambient damage ÷10 again it held at ~3.0 (~30% unique tapes, ~50% related neighbours, programs
flat at ~21 bytes); cutting copy errors as well froze the world into clones (HOE 3.6, 7% unique).
High HOE trades against evolution. `runs/live-m21.toml` is M20 with `birth_bond` 1.0 and ambient damage
÷10 (background and radiation 10⁻⁷). M21 ran 1.21M epochs (606 days). For ~500 days it kept the ancestor's
design (HOE 2.9 → 2.1); then a predator evolved and spread to ~37% of cells:
`TUMBLE PLEN DIGEST PUSH 22 ALLOC LEN CPY H0+ H1+ DUP CAT JMP -6`. It sizes its bite to its neighbour's
tape with `PLEN` (exactly what whole-cell predation requires), raises a child in the freed site, and runs
catalysis inside its copy loop. The other dominant genomes are 8-byte copiers padded with `NOP`s (68% of
all tape bytes were padding, so mean tape length overstates program size). With every birth logged,
`lineage.parquet` grew ~70 GB/day; `telemetry.lineage_every` now thins it (live configs: 1 in 100).

**M22: rewarding work (A–E).** `runs/live-m22.toml` adds all five environment changes (rows above):
passive absorption cut to α = 0.0005 (cells keep ~20% of the light instead of ~95%), a travelling light
band (season 20 days), armour, a two-generation colony life cycle with `light_cover` 0, and computation
tasks. Tuning on 201² (`primordia analyze` now reports executed program size and which behaviours
actually run): with the plain ancestor `IN`/`OUT` never appeared in 15 days; a neutral `IN IN NOP OUT`
stub mutated to everything except the rewarded `SUB`. In head-to-head competitions (half plain, half
`IN IN SUB OUT` ancestors) the task-doers lost when rewards came from the site's field (zero-sum with
passive absorption: larger rewards did worse) or as extra steps, held ~40% with new energy at reward 32,
and won with new energy at reward 128 (50/50 → 56/44 by day 10, ledger exact). M22 is seeded the same
way (`runs/seed-m22.jsonl`), so the live world starts with that competition and harder tasks pay 2–4×
more. (Without NVIDIA's Vulkan driver, `wgpu` silently
falls back to llvmpipe, Mesa's CPU renderer: identical results, ~8 epochs/s. The run manifest's
`gpu_adapter` shows which device ran.)

`runs/live-m1.toml` to `live-m3.toml` are the earlier steps.

**Dormancy sweep (M5).** On a 201² world (2 seeds, 10 days; means over days 6–10), against M4:

* `light_cover` 0 collapsed the world (≈550 cells instead of ≈30,000). Shaded, energy-starved crowded cells
  sit dormant (57% of M4's cells), and dormant cells do not write over their neighbours. With full light
  nearly every cell ran its whole step budget, copy loops eroded, and the founding cohort died at
  `max_age` with nothing to replace it.
* Dormancy penalties from epoch 0 (soft to severe) went extinct: the seeded copiers exhaust themselves
  every slice and died on the first night. Switched on at day 4 without the sleep shield, they gave large,
  more differentiated daytime bodies (mean size 33, 39% with 2+ cell types) but 86% of cells died each
  night and no body lived to dawn. With the shield kept, sleeping alone took over (up to 42% asleep at
  dawn) and bodies shrank.
* Each body change alone was survivable; `s_bond` 0.4 gave the largest and longest-lived bodies, and with
  `share_beta` 0.125 it raised night survival from 60% to 68% and doubled the night-surviving bodies.

The mutation/decay sampler now handles large means without the former overflow/underflow failure.
The 64-event cap still matters experimentally: rate × rot × tape length above that scale is deliberately
truncated rather than allowing unlimited damage work. The historical M5 results above used the earlier
sampler and sleep timing; replay their recorded hashes with their original source revision.

## Telemetry

* **Plot history.** Every live metric is kept as up to 20,000 buckets of mean / min / max over the whole
  run, merged pairwise when full. Plots never drop samples, so the day/night swing cannot alias into false
  trends. The history is saved as `history.json` beside the snapshots and carried over by `--resume`.
* **Day/night bookkeeping.** Sunset is at half the day period and sunrise at 0. At each sunrise the
  recorder logs night survival (sunrise ÷ sunset population), births during the day (exact, from the
  birth stream), deaths (births minus population change), and the asleep / dormant shares and largest
  body at dawn (`days.parquet`).
* **Cluster persistence** (`telemetry.cluster_every`, default 250 epochs; `clusters.parquet`). Two kinds
  of cluster with at least 50 cells are tracked: bonded bodies, and kin clusters (connected patches of
  neighbours whose programs are kin, see below). A cluster continues one from the
  previous sample when at least 25% of the earlier cluster's cells are still alive inside it (same site,
  birth epoch and lineage). A cluster that continues across a sunrise adds a night. Tracking restarts on
  `--resume`.
* **Cell states** (`life.parquet`, every 10 epochs): sunlight, asleep, dormant, SLEEP carriers, bonded,
  mean age, and shares older than one and two days.
* **Body differentiation** (`metrics.parquet`, every 10 epochs). For each bonded body of 10+ cells, cell
  *types* and *roles* are counted. Groups of identical program starts (first 16 tape slots) merge into one
  type when within 4 slots of the type's founding group, largest first, so the many small patches of one
  strain in a big body are one type; roles are members with the same set of behaviour instructions on their
  tape. A type or role counts with 5+ cells. Logged as mean and max per body and the share of bodies with
  two or more. A *kin body* has at least half of its bonds between kin; kin bodies with 2+ types are the
  closest this measure comes to division of labour. (Up to M12 a type needed 5% of the body, so large bodies
  almost never scored; for a while after it, unmerged 5-cell groups made big bodies score hundreds.)
* **Conserved information** (every `entropy_every` epochs): Adami's physical complexity. Cells are grouped
  into families by their first 8 tape bytes; in each family of 20+ cells (up to 200 sampled), every position
  where most members still have a byte and the commonest byte is not empty adds 1 − H/Hmax, with H the
  entropy of that position and Hmax = log₂ of the sample size, capped at 8 bits. Positions kept fixed by
  selection count ~1 and drifting junk ~0, though junk copied recently also looks conserved while damage
  is low. Logged as the abundance-weighted mean and the best family.
* **Executed repertoire** (every `entropy_every` epochs): 400 sampled living programs each run one slice in
  the assay sandbox with a copy of themselves as partner; logged as the mean number of distinct instruction
  kinds executed (PUSH n, JMP ±n and L/D twins counted once; NOP and fillers not at all) and of behaviour
  instructions executed. The sandbox has no neighbours or energy, so it shows what a program does when it
  runs, not what it achieves in the world.
* **High-order entropy** (the Computational Life measure): Shannon entropy of the byte histogram of sampled
  tapes minus Brotli's compressed bits per byte. It is high when the soup is full of copies of shared
  sequences, so it marks the takeover by replicators and falls as lineages diversify or programs shorten;
  it is not a measure of how complex an individual program is.
* **Kinship** (`analysis::kin`, used for kin clusters, kin bodies and kinship across bonds): two programs are
  kin when at least 75% of the filled (non-empty) bytes among the fuller one's first 16 slots match the
  other in place, and it has at least 4. Empty padding never counts, so short programs can be kin and empty
  newborn tapes never are. (Before M14's day 72 padding counted as matching, so in worlds of short programs
  most cells looked related.) Empty tapes are left out of cell types, and pairs with an empty tape are left
  out of kinship across bonds and of a body's kin share.
* **Kinship across bonds** (`kin_bonded`, `kin_unbonded`): the share of bonded and of unbonded occupied
  neighbour pairs that are kin. Bonded pairs no more related than unbonded ones mean bodies are aggregates
  of strangers, and their "cell types" are strains rather than division of labour.
* **Behaviour.** The frequency of 23 behaviour instructions in living programs, relative to a random
  byte (1×), is a live series; the full 256-opcode histogram goes to `opcodes.parquet`.
